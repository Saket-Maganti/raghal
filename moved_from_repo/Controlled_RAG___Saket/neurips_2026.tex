\begin{abstract}
Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) systems are typically evaluated using single configurations of retriever, generator, metric, and threshold, yielding faithfulness claims that are difficult to reproduce and compare across studies. We introduce ControlledRAG, a pre-registered audit framework and minimum reporting standard that exposes seven critical axes of variation in RAG evaluation: generator, retriever, context structure, scorer, human calibration, threshold transfer, and cost. Using a controlled pipeline with fixed generations and matched-context diagnostics, we show that commonly reported improvements are often under-identified: the same outputs can yield substantially different effect sizes across reasonable faithfulness metrics, with low cross-scorer agreement and limited alignment with human judgments. A compact second-generator replication preserves these discrepancies, while threshold-transfer and cost-aware analyses reveal instability across datasets and deployment settings. All experiments are pre-registered and released with auditable per-query artifacts to enable exact reproduction. Rather than proposing a new model or metric, ControlledRAG reframes RAG evaluation as a multi-axis evidence problem and provides a standardized protocol for reporting faithful, reproducible, and comparable results. Code: \url{https://anonymous.4open.science/r/rag-hallucination-detection-MS/README.md}
\end{abstract}
